## Search and selection method

The proposed search covers biomedical KG answering, ontology-grounded retrieval, graph/vector comparisons, evidence verification, and abstention. Candidate search venues include scholarly indexes and publisher/repository pages; the actual venues, query strings, date limits, and inclusion rules require review before the audit. This is not a claim of systematic-review completeness.

Record each search's venue, exact query, date, filters, and screening decisions. Verify bibliographic details against primary publication records and distinguish a full-text assessment from abstract-only screening. Record inaccessible material and uncertainty. Follow relevant references and citing work where useful, documenting how each item was found.

Prioritize work with explicit retrieval mechanisms, datasets, controls, evaluation procedures, or verification definitions relevant to the candidate experiment. Record exclusions and reasons rather than silently discarding inconvenient comparisons. Distinguish original evidence from claims repeated by later papers.

## Future comparison matrix

Create one evidence-backed record per included work with: verified citation and official location; publication version; task/domain; data and question construction; ontology role; graph retrieval mechanism; vector representation; verification target and limits; baselines and controls; held-out policy; metrics; ablations; reported findings with source locations; reproducibility artifacts; limitations; and relevance to the proposed contrast.

Reported findings belong to the cited work and must not be presented as DementiaGraph-V results. Differences in domain or component names alone do not establish novelty. The gap assessment should state what existing evidence already addresses, what remains uncertain, and which proposed experiment could resolve a specific uncertainty. Any novelty claim requires owner approval after this assessment.
