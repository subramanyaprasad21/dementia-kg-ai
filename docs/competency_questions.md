# Competency questions

Status: curation method proposal. No question records or validated answers have been created.

## Three separate question sets

| Set | Purpose and permitted use |
| --- | --- |
| DESIGN COMPETENCY QUESTIONS | Guide ontology scope and information requirements. They influence design and cannot count as unbiased held-out evaluation evidence. |
| DEVELOPMENT QUESTIONS | Support debugging, prompt/configuration development, and tuning. Keep their usage history explicit. |
| HELD-OUT EVALUATION QUESTIONS | Assess the frozen system under approved access rules. They must not become a tuning resource while retaining held-out status. |

The initial target of approximately 25–40 applies to curated design competency questions. It is not a required filler count or a justified evaluation sample size. Set membership, overlap policy, and near-duplicate handling require approval in the evaluation protocol.

## Curation and review

Begin from intended research tasks and inspected source evidence. Draft candidate questions, identify the entities and relationships required to answer them, and check whether the source collection can support the requested conclusion. Revise scope when legitimate questions reveal missing information rather than constructing questions solely to fit a convenient schema.

Candidate families include direct facts, terminology, disease relationships, cross-disease comparisons, multi-hop retrieval, evidence/provenance, ambiguity, insufficient evidence, and invalid requests. Retain families only when justified by scope. Controlled adversarial cases must be labelled as designed fixtures, not source-derived facts.

Each future record should contain an ID, set membership, text, category, expected entities and relationships, expected evidence, answer or answer criteria, reasoning type, expected hops where applicable, answerability, ambiguity, source/provenance, review status, and revision history. Do not fill evidence or review fields by inference. Human review must record what was actually reviewed and by whom, with unresolved disagreements retained.

Before freezing the design set, review coverage, redundancy, scope fit, evidence support, and misleading premises. Expected answers and hop counts are not ground truth until checked under the approved rubric. Reviewer availability, expertise requirements, and adjudication remain REQUIRES HUMAN DECISION.
